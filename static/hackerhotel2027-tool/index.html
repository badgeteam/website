Click or drag to paint. Round dots are the white LEDs, diamonds are the RGB accent LEDs: set Paint to "RGB LEDs" to paint those with a color instead. While painting white LEDs, clicking a diamond opens its own color editor.

RGB LED

A 4-pin RGB accent LED -- driven as three ordinary channels in the same frame, not a separate protocol feature (see docs/LEDPWM_MATRIX.md).

Create animation

Generates a built-in multi-frame pattern -- neon flicker-on, strobe, a chase around the arms, a breathe crossfade, a sparkling second chase, and a strobe finale -- and loads it into the Pattern editor below (Play it there, or send it to the device).

Off: each LED samples the video at its real position on the board (cropped to the board's aspect ratio), so results follow the starfish shape rather than a plain rectangle.

On: the video is scaled to fit the dense circular cluster in the matrix's center (its corners, and depending on the video's aspect ratio some of its left/right content, fall outside that circle and won't show on any LED). Every other LED -- out along the arms -- instead takes on the color of whichever edge of that circle it points away from, like a Philips Ambilight TV.

A small, dim 384-LED display reads best with a brightness boost and high-contrast source footage.

Renders the text at high resolution and slides it across only the dense circular cluster of LEDs at the board's center, so it reads as a proper dot-matrix marquee rather than a squashed rectangle. The sparser LEDs running out along the arms -- plain white ones and the diamond-shaped RGB accents -- are too sparse to carry a legible glyph, so they're left free for their own effect instead of showing (badly stretched) text.

Speed is a fraction of one full chase/blink cycle per frame -- higher wraps around (or blinks) faster. Twinkle ignores speed and just sparkles randomly. Ignored if a group's effect is Off.

Help

The page sends the badge into its USB boot ROM and asks you to pick it ("Unknown device from QinHeng Electronics"). Without a connected badge: on the badge, hold A for the menu, press C to reach UPDATE, and tap A. Your items, name and settings stay.

Linux: the udev rule from the README, for 1a86:55e0 too. Windows: once, install the WinUSB driver for the boot ROM with Zadig: Device > Create New Device, USB ID 1A86 55E0, driver WinUSB, Install Driver. Without it the boot ROM never shows up in the list. Firefox and Safari have no WebUSB: use Chrome, Edge or Opera, or flash with minichlink -w ledmatrix.bin flash -b.

If an update stops half way, the badge starts in its boot ROM: update again. If it no longer starts at all, unplug it (and its battery) and plug it in again, or bring it to the badge recovery area at the event.

On the badge

Apps, animations and texts stored on the badge. The one marked ★ starts at power-on; hold any button while the badge powers on to skip it. On the badge, hold A for the menu (also from an app).

    Connect to see what is stored.

    Add a text

    The badge shows the text in its own font, as the name tag: still if it fits, else scrolling. It stores one byte per letter, so a long text fits (an animation of it would not). Letters outside plain ASCII lose their accents or show as ?.

    App library: the apps that come with the firmware. Add stores one on the badge and starts it; Update replaces an older copy (its saved data, such as high scores, stays).
